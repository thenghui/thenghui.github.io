## Verdict

All eight respondents rated the training 4 or 5 and none scored it below 4, on both overall quality and trainer effectiveness (4.25 / 5 each). There is no dissatisfaction to answer. The finding that matters is narrower: six of the eight stopped at 4 rather than 5, and half the room called the content only "somewhat relevant" to their job. The training was delivered well and landed one step short of the participants' own work.

## Key Takeaways & Recommendations

*Written for Amsteel's programme owner: what this data supports, what it does not, and what to decide before the next intake.*

1. **The pilot cleared its gate — repeat the intake, not the syllabus.** Eight of eight rated the session 4 or 5 with no score below 4, and trainer effectiveness matched at 4.25 / 5. Nothing here justifies a redesign before the next cohort — but an identical repeat adds familiarity without adding capability.

2. **Relevance is your adoption risk, and the cheapest fix on this page.** Four of eight answered "somewhat relevant" and nobody answered "no": your developers followed the material but did not connect it to Amsteel's repositories, review rules and release process. Left alone, that surfaces later as licences nobody uses. Fix it inside the existing agenda — 20 minutes where each participant applies the assistant to a live ticket and walks the room through the diff. No new content, no extra day.

3. **Buy the governance module before the advanced one.** Cybersecurity and DPO obligations drew 2 requests, tied as the joint-largest future topic. This is a policy question, not a training preference: your team is already pasting code and data into assistants and is asking for the rules. A 1-day *AI for Secure Development & Compliance* session for IT plus the DPO and PDPA owner — what may leave the network, what must never be pasted, and review gates for AI-written code — carries the clearest risk reduction here.

4. **Protect the format, and fund the artefact that outlives it.** Tools and tips (3), hands-on work (2) and real-life examples (2) were named by seven of eight respondents; only one mentioned delivery style. Do not let a cheaper lecture or e-learning substitute replace the demo-then-lab structure, and fund a take-away prompt workbook so the exercises still work when the trainer and the internet connection are gone.

5. **Commission the evidence this survey cannot give you.** Eight responses from one session, with no before-and-after measurement, can tell you the room was satisfied — not that your team now writes better code. Add a 30-day follow-up before the next budget cycle: a three-question pulse on weekly assistant use, plus a count of AI-assisted pull requests from your Git host. Those two numbers, not this satisfaction score, are what justify rolling the programme out beyond IT.
