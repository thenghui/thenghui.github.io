# Script patterns for a Bahasa Malaysia imaging sequence

## 1. Length maths

At 118 BPM one bar is **2.034 s** and a four-bar phrase is **8.136 s**. Build every element as one
phrase:

| Slot | Budget | Spoken words (BM) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Voice in an 8.1 s element | 5.5–6.5 s | 14–19 words |
| Sweeper inside music | 6–7 s | 16–20 words |
| Short ID | 3.5–4.5 s | 9–12 words |
| Final tag | 3–4 s | 6–9 words |

Leave 1–2 s at the end of each phrase for the designed exit. A read that fills the whole 8 s gives
the exit nowhere to land.

## 2. The six elements

### Station identification

Purpose: name, promise, slogan. Heard once an hour minimum.
Shape: **[Station]. [Promise]. [Slogan].**

> "Hot FM. Muzik yang hangat, sepanjang hari. `<short pause>` Yang Terhangat, Yang Terbaik."

Do: put the station name in the first two words.
Don't: open with a claim ("Nomor satu...") the listener cannot verify.

### Sweeper

Purpose: make the station feel local and personal while music is already playing.
Shape: **[Observation about the listener]. [Agreement]. [Station]. [Invitation].**

> "Korang tengah drive pergi kerja, kan? `<short pause>` Sama la kita. Hot FM, kawan perjalanan
> korang. Jom, layan."

Do: use first person ("aku", "kita") and one question.
Don't: describe the music ("lagu yang best") — the listener can hear it.

### Short station ID

Purpose: re-anchor identity between songs, 3–5 s.
Shape: **[Station]! [Who it belongs to]. [What it delivers].**

> "Hot FM! Ini station korang. Lagu terbaik, setiap jam."

Do: name the station twice if it fits (head and mid).
Don't: add a second idea — this element is a flag, not a sentence.

### TOH / hour opener

Purpose: mark the hour, promise the next one.
Shape: **[Time check]. [What is coming]. [Station].**

> "Dah pukul lapan pagi, korang. `<short pause>` Satu jam muzik tanpa henti, cerita paling panas,
> semua ada kat sini."

Do: keep the time check as the first clause so it can be swapped live.
Don't: stack three promises; one music promise plus one content promise is enough.

### Promo sting

Purpose: one tease, one action.
Shape: **[When]. [What]. [Do this].**

> "Esok pagi ada kejutan besar untuk korang. `<short pause>` Jangan miss, tau!"

Do: name a real show, prize or feature — **verified**.
Don't: invent a show name, a cash amount, or a guest. If it is not confirmed, write "ada kejutan
besar" and let the promo producer fill the detail.

### Final tag into music

Purpose: last identity, then get out of the way.
Shape: **[Station]. [Slogan].**

> "Hot FM. Yang Terhangat, Yang Terbaik."

Do: place it 1–2 s before the music clearance, not on the downbeat.
Don't: add a call to action — the music is the call to action.

## 3. Claim discipline

| Safe | Needs a source | Never invent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "muzik tanpa henti" (format promise) | "nomor satu" (ratings claim) | show names |
| "lagu terbaik" (opinion) | "paling ramai dengar" | artist appearances |
| "kawan perjalanan korang" (positioning) | listener numbers | prize values |
| station slogan **if supplied** | award counts | dates and venues |

If the client supplies a slogan, quote it exactly — do not "improve" the wording.

## 4. Time-check variants

| Clock | BM line |
| --- | --- |
| 06:00 | "Dah pukul enam pagi, korang." |
| 08:00 | "Dah pukul lapan pagi, korang." |
| 12:00 | "Tengah hari dah, korang." |
| 17:00 | "Dah pukul lima petang." |
| 22:00 | "Dah pukul sepuluh malam." |

Record in the script that the demo uses a fixed time and the live read replaces it.

## 5. What a good read sounds like

- One breath mark per sentence, at the `<short pause>`.
- The station name is the loudest, clearest word in the line.
- The last three words slow down; they are the ones the listener remembers.
- No rising terminal on statements — Malaysian radio ends statements flat or falling.
