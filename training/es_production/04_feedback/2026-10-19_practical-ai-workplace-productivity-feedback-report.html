Key Takeaways & Recommendations

  1. Top-line result. All 18 participants rated the training 4 or 5 — 13 at full marks — for an average of 4.72 / 5, and trainer effectiveness scored 4.67 / 5. Nothing fell below 4, so the session met expectations across the room.
  2. Strongest element. Tools and techniques (5 mentions) lead hands-on practice (4) and real-life examples (4). The lab format is the product: protect it, and add a take-away workbook so participants can repeat the workflows without live internet.
  3. Gap to close. Six of 18 called the content only "somewhat relevant"; one in three did not see their own job in the material. Run examples by function (finance/accounting, HR/admin, operations, creative) and ask each participant to bring one real task to work on.
  4. Demand for the next level. Requests tie three ways at 4 mentions: free or low-cost tools, applying AI to daily tasks, and data analysis/reporting. A follow-up day with one data-and-reporting module, a no-licence-cost tool stack, and a role track for accounting/tax, finance/procurement and video answers the top three at once.
  5. Cheap wins from the margins. Record the sessions ("Record the skil"), add a game-based recap (one request for "more games"), and publish a one-page glossary of tool names — one answer, "AI GROB BOK", suggests the tool names are not yet sticking.
