Key Takeaways & Recommendations

Overall reception is strong. Both overall quality (4.16/5) and trainer effectiveness (4.09/5) are solidly above the midpoint, and 96% found content at least somewhat relevant.
Hands-on is the hook. Practical exercises were the single most-valued element (44 mentions). Future sessions should keep — and expand — the hands-on format, ideally with step-by-step slide guides provided alongside live demos (multiple respondents cited unstable internet making live follow-along difficult).
Relevance gap to close. 43% rated content only "somewhat relevant." Segmenting future sessions by function (clinic operations, finance/accounting, design/marketing, content creation) would lift relevance toward full "Yes."
Clear demand for the next level. Beyond the undecided third, the strongest demand is for AI applied to daily work/productivity (16), followed by domain-specific applications (clinic, design, e-commerce), data analysis/Excel (10), and advanced/deep-dive AI techniques (9). A suggested follow-up curriculum:
Module A
AI for daily productivity & workflow automation (broad)
Module B
Role-based tracks — clinic operations; finance & reporting; design & content
Module C
Advanced track — AI agents/agentic AI, prompt engineering, intro to coding/GitHub
Listen to the dissenter. The lone 2/2 rating ("lecture not meet my expectations") specifically wanted AI to interpret clinic sales and dispensing data — a concrete, buildable use case that should be addressed in a clinic-operations track.
